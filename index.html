What if rates change?

Banks approve a loan by checking you could still pay it at a rate 3 points above your actual rate (APRA rule). The “bank test” row shows that repayment.

How it's calculated

  • Repayment: principal and interest (P&I) loan, variable rate held constant for the whole term, paid monthly. Fortnightly and weekly show the equivalent amount.
  • Stamp duty: Queensland Revenue Office rates for contracts from 1 Aug 2026. Established first home: exempt up to A$ 700k, partial concession up to A$ 800k. New first home: exempt, no cap. Temporary resident or foreign buyer: full rates plus 8% AFAD.
  • LMI: average of insurer rate cards (Helia and QBE) plus 9% QLD duty on the premium. A real quote can differ by a few thousand. Many lenders let you add LMI to the loan instead of paying it upfront.
  • 5% Deposit Scheme: for first home buyers who are citizens or permanent residents and will live in the home, with a A$ 1M price cap on the Gold Coast, Brisbane and Sunshine Coast.
  • Approval: the bank tests the repayment at a rate 3 points higher (APRA rule) and deducts 2026-27 income tax and Medicare, living expenses (the higher of yours and HEM), other debts and 3.8% of card limits. The minimum also keeps DTI at 6 times income or less, since few loans above that get approved. For a couple with two incomes, income is split equally. Every bank has its own formula, so treat this as a range.
  • Comfortable: repayment at today's rate of no more than 30% of gross income, the usual line for mortgage stress.
  • Not included: First Home Owner Grant, FIRB fee, home insurance, council rates, body corporate and moving costs.
  • This is a planning estimate, not financial advice. Bank approval also depends on your income, expenses and other debts. Check figures with the Queensland Revenue Office, your lender or a broker.
